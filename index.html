The false sense of security

Why many tax firms assume they are compliant — but are not.

“Our IT provider handles everything.”

General IT support may keep systems running, but WISP compliance also requires written policies, employee procedures, documented safeguards, and ongoing review.

“We are too small to be a target.”

Small tax practices hold high-value data and often have fewer security controls than larger firms.

“Our software is in the cloud.”

Cloud software does not secure the laptop, identity, email account, or local access path used to reach it.

“We bought a WISP template.”

A generic template does not implement MFA, training, encryption, backups, or annual review. The plan has to reflect the real business.

Audit scope

What WispCheck evaluates.

The assessment is organized around the safeguard areas tax firms are expected to address.

WISP foundation & structure

Written plan, security coordinator, ownership, and review schedule.

Administrative controls

Employee training, seasonal staff practices, procedures, and documentation.

Technical safeguards

Encryption, endpoint protection, system logging, and backup readiness.

Access controls & credentials

MFA, passwords, account security, and secure access to taxpayer information.

Risk & vendor oversight

Risk assessment, third-party oversight, and security obligations around vendors.
