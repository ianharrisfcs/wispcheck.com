THE BIG MISUNDERSTANDING

A WISP is not just a document.

A useful WISP describes the security program your firm actually operates: who owns it, what information you protect, what risks you face, what safeguards are in place, how vendors are handled, and what happens when something goes wrong.

▤

“We bought a template”

A template can help organize a plan, but it cannot implement MFA, training, encryption, access reviews, backups, incident response, vendor oversight or annual review.

♧

“Our IT provider handles security”

IT support may implement controls, but the firm still needs a written program, assigned responsibility, documented decisions and evidence that safeguards are maintained.

☁

“Everything is in the cloud”

Cloud software does not eliminate risks from email, identities, local devices, remote access, staff behavior, vendors or stolen credentials.

♢

“We are too small to matter”

Smaller practices still handle highly sensitive taxpayer data. The program can be scaled to the firm, but it should still be real, written and maintained.

ASSESSMENT SCOPE

What the diagnostic reviews.

The tool is intentionally broader than a simple “Do you have a WISP?” checklist.

Program foundation

Customized written plan, Qualified Individual / coordinator, review cycle and management accountability.

Risk & data inventory

Written risk assessment, data locations, systems, devices, users and material changes.

Identity & access

MFA, least privilege, account lifecycle, privileged access and periodic access review.

Technical safeguards

Endpoint security, patching, encryption, logging, monitoring, backup and recovery testing.

People & physical security

Training, seasonal staff practices, clean handling of paper records and secure disposal.

Vendors & incident response

Service-provider review, contractual safeguards, written response planning and post-incident improvement.

FAQ

Common questions.

Do tax and accounting professionals really need a WISP?

The IRS currently tells tax professionals that a Written Information Security Plan is required and points firms to Publication 5708/5709 and FTC Safeguards Rule guidance. WispCheck links directly to the official sources so you can verify the requirement yourself.

Does a high WispCheck score mean we are compliant?

No. The readiness score is based only on your self-reported answers. It is an educational screening tool, not a certification, regulatory audit, penetration test or legal opinion.

Is a WISP just a policy template?

No. The written document should reflect an actual program: assigned ownership, risk assessment, safeguards, training, vendor oversight, testing, incident response and ongoing updates appropriate to the firm.

Do I need to register first?

No. The assessment runs in your browser and produces the report immediately. Your answers are stored locally in the browser unless you intentionally contact HarrisFCS.

Can HarrisFCS help implement the gaps?

Yes. HarrisFCS can assist with WISP development and review, security controls, MFA, endpoint protection, backup, training, cloud administration, vendor documentation and ongoing managed IT/security support.
