THE FALSE SENSE OF SECURITY

Why many tax firms assume they are compliant
(but aren't)

WISP compliance under the IRS and the FTC is about written documentation and
verifiable safeguards — areas where general IT setups routinely fall short.

♧

“Our local IT provider handles everything”

Standard IT support ensures your computers boot and your printers work. It does not necessarily document or maintain the safeguards required by your WISP.

♢

“We are too small to be a target”

Cybercriminals actively target small accounting offices because they hold valuable taxpayer data and often have fewer formal controls.

☁

“Our software is in the cloud”

Cloud software does not secure the laptop, identity, email account, or local access path used to reach it.

▤

“We bought a WISP template”

A template does not implement MFA, training, encryption, backups, or annual review. The plan must reflect the real business.

AUDIT SCOPE

What WispCheck evaluates.

The assessment is organized around safeguard areas tax firms are expected to address.

WISP foundation & structure

Written plan, security coordinator, ownership, and review schedule.

Administrative controls

Employee training, seasonal staff practices, procedures, and documentation.

Technical safeguards

Encryption, endpoint protection, system logging, and backup readiness.

Access controls & credentials

MFA, passwords, account security, and secure access to taxpayer information.

Risk & vendor oversight

Risk assessment, third-party oversight, and vendor security obligations.
